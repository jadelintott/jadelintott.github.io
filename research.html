Research

Probability, combinatorics, and rare events in random graphs.

My research studies rare events in random discrete structures, with a particular focus on subgraph counts, conditioned structure, and sampling in sparse random graphs.

Overview

Research summary

My research lies at the intersection of probability, combinatorics, and sampling. I am especially interested in rare events in sparse Erdos-Renyi random graphs, where lower-tail questions about subgraph counts bring together sharp asymptotic estimates, structural combinatorics, and algorithmic ideas.

In current work on triangle counts, I study how often atypical low-count events occur, what overlapping configurations realize them, and how conditioned graphs can be sampled efficiently. More broadly, I aim to understand how exact asymptotics, conditioned geometry, and sampling methods reinforce one another across random combinatorial models.

Current themes

  • Lower tails and large deviations for subgraph counts
  • Conditioned structure in sparse random graphs
  • Sampling methods for rare-event random graph models

Projects

Current and recent projects

Current work

Lower tails for triangle counts

I study exact lower-tail asymptotics for triangle counts in sparse random graphs, including the transition from Poisson-like behavior to overlap-driven corrections.

Broader program

Conditioned geometry and sampling

A central goal of my work is to describe the structures that realize rare events and use those descriptions to design efficient approximate sampling methods.

Next directions

Beyond triangles

I am interested in extending these ideas to more general subgraphs and to wider families of sparse random structures.
